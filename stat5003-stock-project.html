Dataset assessment

Kaggle S&P 500 Stocks is large, messy, and easy to model.

The dataset combines daily stock prices, an S&P 500 index series, and company-level descriptors. It is big enough for robust machine-learning splits, imperfect enough to require cleaning, and structured enough to produce a clear classification target.

What is inside

Version 1023 contains 1,891,536 stock-date rows, 2,517 index rows, and 502 company rows. The stock file runs from 2010-01-04 to 2024-12-20; the index file runs from 2014-12-22 to 2024-12-20.

Course-requirement fit

Large

Nearly 1.9 million stock-date rows support time splits, validation sets, and enough training examples for tree-based models.

Messy

Stocks enter and leave the effective history, many symbol-date combinations are absent, and leakage risk appears when current company information is joined to old prices.

Integrated

The final modelling table can merge price behaviour, market movement, and company or sector descriptors.

Limits to state honestly

  • It should not be presented as fully Complex unless the group adds more difficult feature engineering or modelling structure.
  • Using today's S&P 500 company list for older years introduces survivorship bias.
  • Company-level fields are current snapshots, so historical modelling must avoid leaking future information.
  • The high-volatility threshold still needs a local class-balance calculation before it becomes final.
02

Research question

Can recent stock behaviour predict high volatility 20 trading days ahead?

For each stock on each date, we can build lagged features from past returns, volume, market movement, and sector information, then predict whether the next 20 trading days become a high-volatility period.

Target construction

Forward 20-day realised volatility

Compute daily log returns after date t, take the standard deviation over the next 20 trading days, annualise it, then compare it with a threshold.

vol20(t) = sd(r[t+1:t+20]) x sqrt(252)
Threshold status

30% annualised volatility is only a provisional working threshold. The next data step is to count positives at several candidate thresholds and choose a level with a usable class balance.

Why this question is likely to produce usable model results

  • Volatility has persistence: recent volatility is often informative about near-future volatility.
  • Tree models can capture nonlinear relationships between lagged returns, volume changes, market movement, and sector.
  • The target is naturally generated from the dataset, so the task does not require a separate labelled source.
  • The group can tune the threshold to avoid an extremely rare positive class.

Candidate feature set

Price

Lagged returns, rolling mean returns, rolling volatility, drawdown, high-low range, open-close range.

Volume

Log volume, rolling volume change, abnormal volume versus the last 20 or 60 trading days.

Market

Lagged S&P 500 returns and market volatility as broad risk context.

Sector

Sector and industry indicators, used carefully to avoid leaking current-only information.

Models and evaluation

  • Start with Logistic Regression as the interpretable baseline.
  • Use Decision Tree and Random Forest for stronger nonlinear performance.
  • Use KNN or SVM on a sampled/standardised subset if runtime is high.
  • Evaluate with a time-based split, a 20-day purge gap, ROC-AUC, F1, recall, and confusion matrices.

Next preprocessing decisions

Calibrate labels

Calculate positive counts for candidate annualised thresholds such as 20%, 25%, 30%, 35%, and percentile cutoffs such as top 20% or top 30%.

Decide class count

If the assignment requires at least three classes, define low / medium / high volatility by quantiles, for example bottom 30%, middle 40%, top 30%.

Prevent leakage

Only use features available at date t; remove rows whose 20-day forward window crosses the train/test boundary.
